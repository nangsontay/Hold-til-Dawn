# Hold 'til Dawn (prototype)

Godot **4.7.2 .NET** with the **.NET 8 SDK** (or a compatible newer SDK). All game scripts, tests and art tools are C#. Rules from the GDD live in the `GameSim` class (server only); the `Main` class is network, input, drawing and HUD. Both are split into partial files per team member; each file starts with an `Owner:` comment.

## Run
- Install the [Godot .NET editor and .NET SDK](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/c_sharp/c_sharp_basics.html), then run `dotnet build`. The regular Godot editor cannot run C# projects. Commands below use the .NET Godot executable as `godot`.
- **Play:** open the project in Godot, press Play, choose a role, click **PLAY**, add bot teammates in the lobby, then **Start**.
- **Multiplayer is removed for now** so the team can finish the gameplay first. The networked version (ENet host/join, dedicated server, network test) is kept in git commit `cf44b5f` ("Port to C#"). `GameSim` still exposes the same API (`AddPlayer`, `RemovePlayer`, `Input`, `Action`, `Tick`, `Snapshot`) so the network layer can be added back on top.
- The lobby leader (first to join) picks the mode and presses **Start** (needs at least one Engineer).

## Controls
WASD move, mouse aim, LMB attack with the selected weapon (bows shoot toward the cursor), E (hold) gather / repair door or tower (Engineer, needs the hammer) / revive / pick up a dropped item, 1-4 or mouse wheel select a hotbar slot, Tab inventory and (at the workbench) base storage with drag and drop, G give the selected stack to a teammate in reach or drop it, Q bandage, C workbench (number keys or click; each craft takes 3 s), B build menu (Engineer: wood wall, stone wall, tower; R turns a wall, LMB places, RMB cancels), RMB healing flask (Healer), F (hold 2 s, Engineer at night) enter or leave the base through a closed door, V skip-day vote, T quick-place a tower at your feet, M large map (the minimap in the bottom right follows you), Ctrl + wheel zoom, Esc pause menu (closes open panels first).

Day-skip vote (FML-14): press V by day to vote or withdraw. Votes count once 180 s of the day have passed; the day then ends when at least 60% of the people vote (bots do not vote; one vote when playing solo). A day lasts at most 360 s. The 15-second dusk warning (BAL-05) follows, then night. For a quick test, the debug-only F9 key (host) ends the current phase.

## What is in
- 5 day/night cycles on an 11 x 7 tile base (GDD: 15 x 10) with doors A (west), B (north), C (east), no south door; players enter only through doors, and at night only the Engineer (hold F) or anyone through a broken door.
- Players, enemies and bots share scenery/resource/building colliders from `src/world/WorldGeometry.cs`. Movement is swept in small steps; the server validates player movement and acknowledges predicted inputs. Grass, pebbles, bushes and herbs stay walk-through. Enemies and bots navigate around obstacles with cached A* paths (pressed against a solid, they slide toward the next path cell instead of stopping).
- Bot teammates (`src/ai/Bots.cs`, not in the GDD, for demos and solo tests): the lobby leader adds or removes them (max 5 players); a bot takes the missing role (Engineer, then Healer, Scout). They run on the server with the same rules as people: dodge boss slams, revive downed teammates, Healer flasks, fight, Engineer door repair, gather by day and craft near dusk, hold the attacked door at night. Only people count for the day-skip vote.
- Night waves spawn out of sight 25-35 tiles out in a band in front of each attacked door (GDD 4.3, BAL-34). First wave after 30 s, then every 90 s (45 s on night 5) (BAL-04); budget from FML-04. Enemy speeds follow BAL-29..32 (Grunt and Spitter 2 tiles/s, Wrecker 1.6, x1.08 per night, cap 3); a boss moves like a Grunt of its night. Boss on nights 2 and 4, night-5 helicopter extraction.
- Daytime roamers beyond 20 tiles from the base (chance grows with distance per FML-16, pairs, max 8); at dusk they walk to an attacked door.
- Enemy drops (BAL-41: Grunt 10%, Spitter 15%, Wrecker 20%, boss always; resource nodes 4 + 3n per type, BAL-17): walk over to pick up, gone after 30 s, not from inside the base.
- Defence towers (AGT-12, MEC-11, MEC-28, BAL-28): 300 HP, shoot the nearest enemy within 6 tiles at 8 DPS, light 6 tiles at night for everyone. One free tower (placed from the build menu (B) or with T by an Engineer on day 1, or it appears in front of night 1's door at dusk); more cost 10 wood + 5 titanium after the first boss; one per door. At night enemies walking into a tower's light attack it first (Wreckers x2, Spitters never); the Engineer repairs it like a door, a destroyed one only to 30% and only with no enemy in its light.
- Inventory (MEC-09, BAL-18): 12 slots of up to 20 (weapons and armour 1 per slot), the first 4 are the hotbar; 40 shared storage slots in the storage chest by the north wall (east of door B; stand at it and press Tab, its lid opens as anyone walks up; Tab elsewhere opens only your own bag, and the bag opened at the chest closes as you walk away from it). The crafted bow holds its own arrows (up to 30, shown on its slot): arrows you get go into it first, dragging arrows onto it loads them, shots use loose arrows first, and a dropped or given bow takes its arrows along. Kits (team change to BAL-42, no basic weapon item): Scout a starter bow (the crafted bow's 12 damage, 1 shot/s and 8-tile range, but no arrows needed), Engineer the hammer, Healer empty hands and 3 flasks. With no weapon in the selected slot a player fights bare-handed with the GDD basic weapon's numbers (15 damage, 1.5/s, reach 1.5 tiles). The best armour carried is worn. Player-dropped items stay until picked up with E; enemy drops need free space.
- Crafting (MEC-10, BAL-19, section 3.2): 3 s per item standing at the workbench (by the north wall, west of door B; C), cancelled by walking away or a hit; C at the workbench opens the workbench tab over the whole screen (see-through, the HUD hidden; it will not open elsewhere and closes once you are half a tile past the bench reach): every recipe as a card with its icon and cost, by tier, and the picked one in detail (what it does, its numbers, how it is made as ingredients -> result, the steps) with a Craft button (`ui/gameplay/Main.CraftPanel.cs`), while a number key still crafts at once; recipes include Bow + 5 arrows and 5 arrows (stone tier after night 1). Base repair is Engineer-only, by day, 20 HP/s. A Revival kit brings a dead teammate back at the door nearest to where they fell.
- Walls (ITM-07, ITM-11, MEC-11, BAL-27): wood (5 wood, 200 HP) and stone (5 stone, 400 HP, after night 1), 3 tiles long, not within 3 tiles of a door; enemies walk around them and attack one that blocks them.
- Bosses (AGT-05..11, table 4.2): all seven archetypes; night 2 draws one at random and night 4 another (FML-05), announced at dawn. Each has the shared slam plus its move: Juggernaut charge, Broodmother summons, Bio-Cannon projectile ring, Burrower burrow, Gatebreaker 120-point door blow, Phantom cloak and ambush, Ironhide front armour. Every damaging move has a 1.0 s ground telegraph.
- HUD: phase and timer (top right), generator and doors (top), team list (left), HP, armour % and bandages (bottom left), hotbar (bottom), minimap (top left: the island around you with the routes, trails, numbered landmarks, base, doors A B C by state, forest and rocks, resource nodes still holding resources, towers, teammates, you and the camera view; no enemies, GDD 6.1). M opens the island map, laid out like the level-design map: the 18 numbered landmarks with a list of their names, the main gathering routes, exploration trails, safe zone, night spawn bands, boss spawns, landing pads and the bays. Walking into a landmark's area shows its name under the radio line for a few seconds. Damage per player is on the result screen.
- Result screen (section 2.5): win or loss with its cause (e.g. "Generator destroyed at door B"), night reached and per-player kills, revives, damage dealt and taken; Back to lobby. Esc opens a pause overlay (a solo match pauses).

## 2.5D look (branch `proto-2.5d`)
- The rules are still flat 2D (`GameSim` unchanged); only `Main` draws differently: everything with height (trees, rocks, walls, enemies, players) is sorted by the y of its foot, walls have a top and a front face, and bodies stand above their shadows. Scenery and resource nodes stay opaque even when a character stands behind them; a gathered node shows what is left of it (stump, rubble, cut stems, broken crystals) until dawn.
- Pixel-art world (GDD style guide): everything is drawn without smoothing, and the camera zoom keeps one art pixel on a whole number of screen pixels.
- Fonts in `fonts/` (GDD table 6.1; SIL OFL 1.1, licence files next to them, from the Google Fonts repository): **Noto Sans** (Google, weight 600) for all text, **Oswald** (Vernon Adams and contributors, weight 600) for titles, the timer, big numbers and banners. The UI is drawn at the window resolution, so the letters stay sharp.
- Base props in `art/props/` (lighthouse = beacon, workbench (2 tiles wide), storage chest (4 frames, shut to open), tower, wrecked tower, landing pad) are pixel art drawn by `tools/PixelProps.cs`; doors have lamp pillars that light up at dusk. The base itself is drawn in code (`src/world/view/Main.BaseLook.cs`): weathered cream concrete walls with pilasters, rust runs and moss at the foot, a stone-paved floor, and warm lamps (door pillars, two sconces on the north wall) that light it at night. The world light is a warm afternoon sun by day and a deep teal night where only the lamps, the beacon and tower lights glow orange; the base stands on the grass; no letters are drawn at the doors (the HUD door bars name them).
- Nature sprites in `art/nature/` (pines, dead trees, rock outcrops, shrubs, grass tufts, pebbles, the four resource nodes and their gathered remains, the ground texture) are pixel art at 1 px = 1 game px drawn by `tools/PixelNature.cs`; titanium nodes are dark slate rocks grown with blue and violet crystals that pulse and twinkle in game (`node_titanium_glow_*` is the crystal layer), so they never pass for the pale grey stone (GDD 4.3). See `art/nature/CREDITS.txt`.
- The island follows the level-design map "Hold 'til Dawn - Island Map", drawn 1.5 times its size (`IslandMap.SCALE`: about 230 x 210 tiles; 18 landmarks, 3 main gathering routes). Only the island grows: the base, its landing pads, the 20-tile safe zone, the night spawn bands and the boss distance keep their sizes, and the wood, stone and herb clusters by the outpost move in to stay inside the safe zone. `tools/island_from_pdf.py <pdf>` reads the PDF (its map picture pixel by pixel, its routes, trails and landmark numbers by their coordinates) and writes `maps/island_map.json`: the coast, the inner edge of the 4-6 tile beaches, the shallows, the hills, the woods, one point per tree, the routes, trails, landmarks and bays, in tiles from the outpost. `src/world/IslandMap.cs` loads it for every peer: the sea is impassable (players, enemies, bosses and bots slide along the coast; the bots' A* grid marks the sea solid), spawns are pulled inland, and scenery grows from the map's trees and woods. Terrain (`src/world/WorldTerrain.cs`, drawn by `src/world/view/Main.TerrainView.cs`): the sea stepping up through the shallows, the beach with a wet edge and rolling surf, grass lighter in the safe zone, darker woodland floor, walkable hills drawn with contour steps, the main routes, exploration trails and dirt roads out along the three door axes to the boss spawns, the Rainwater Pond (15) and the Mangrove Swamp (08: mud, wet patches and pools with reeds), rock spires round the Southwest Cave (16). Ponds and pools block movement but not arrows or spit; spires block both. Resource nodes sit at the landmarks: wood at 02 Coconut Grove, stone at 03 Limestone Flats (and 16), herbs at 04 Herb Meadow (and 15, 18), three in four of each in the safe zone; titanium spread over the five salvage sites 05, 06, 07, 13 and 14. Night enemies spawn in a 10 x 10 tile band 25-35 tiles out on each active door's axis; bosses 60 tiles out. The base and its landing pads are unchanged.
- Landmarks are built after the concept art of the "Hold 'til Dawn - Island Landmark Atlas" (one picture per landmark in `art/landmarks/ref/`, kept out of the Godot import by `.gdignore`). `tools/PixelLandmarks.cs` (part of the PixelNature tool) draws their pixel art into `art/landmarks/` + `index.json`, and `src/world/WorldLandmarks.cs` places it from the island's shape, the same on every peer: coconut palms, a fallen trunk and dry branches in the Coconut Grove (02); low limestone shelves (03); broad-leaf herbs with purple spikes along the meadow trail (04); the military utility vehicle slid off the road (05); the Ruined Base's concrete floor, broken walls with rebar and vines, shelves, crates and radio mast (06); three convoy trucks beside their track, one with its canvas torn open on crates (07); mangroves on stilt roots round the swamp pools (08); the weather station with its wind vane and aerial (09); the Old Fishing Pier out over the sea with fish traps on the beach (10, walkable: `IslandMap.Docks`); stone steps up the Northwind Slope between rocky shoulders (11); the tin-roofed lookout on stilts over a coral reef (12); the Container Yard's concrete, containers (stacked, end-on with its doors open) drums and pallets (13); the cargo ship aground on the south-east beach, its hull split open, machinery washed up round it (14); red ginger and lily pads at the Rainwater Pond (15); the limestone bluff over the cave mouth (16); the concrete lookout platform at the tip of the south cape (17); raised beds, a broken fence and a watering can in the Abandoned Herb Garden (18). Standing structures are depth-sorted scenery with solid circles; floors, the pier, the platform, steps, coral and lilies lie on the ground layer (`src/world/view/Main.LandmarkView.cs`). The minimap shows them.
- Characters, enemies and bosses have 8 facing directions (sheet rows: right, down, left, up, down-right, down-left, up-left, up-right); the diagonals are 3/4 views (players and enemies: the front / back pixel map with the face redrawn turned, in `tools/PixelChars.cs`; bosses: built by `tools/PixelTurn.cs`, which never cuts through an eye). Both eyes keep the front view's size in the diagonals. The facing changes only once the angle is clearly past the edge between two directions, so diagonal walking does not flicker.
- Players and enemies in `art/chars/` are 32x32 pixel art (Wrecker 40x40) of the GDD characters (CHR-01..03 Scout, Healer, Engineer; CHR-05..07 Grunt, Spitter, Wrecker), drawn by `tools/PixelChars.cs` (pixel maps, GDD palette, outline, 8 directions, idle + walk); see `art/chars/CREDITS.txt`. Each sheet cell has 8 px of room around the body (48x48, Wrecker 56x56; `chars.json` gives the cell `size` and the character's own `body` size). After idle and walk, the sheets have fight and knock-down frames from `tools/PixelPoses.cs` (listed under `anims` in `chars.json` / `bosses.json`, 4 frames each): players one attack per weapon (bare-hands jab, stone-spear thrust, titanium-blade slash with a smear, overhead hammer smash with dust and cracks, bow draw and release), the Grunt a claw rake, the Spitter a spit, the Wrecker a two-fist ground smash, each boss a wind-up / slam / impact / recover; then everyone a fall (hit, knees give, falling, lying with the eyes shut). The arm, weapon and effect are posed in 3D around the body and projected to each of the 8 views; seen from the back the body hides them. The PNGs can be repainted by hand. A coloured ring at a player's feet shows the role.

## Dev
- C# solution: `HoldTilDawn.sln`; build with `dotnet build`. Run all regression checks with `powershell -File tools/Test.ps1 -Godot <path-to-Godot-.NET-console.exe>` (geometry and rules; the network suite is in git commit `cf44b5f` ("Port to C#")). The full simulation suite advances many minutes of game time and can take several minutes.
- Settings (one file per owner, edit in Godot's Inspector, restart the game after a change): `config/gameplay/gameplay.tres` (vote delay, dusk), `config/ai/ai.tres` (enemy speed, wave timing, spawn/roamer/drop multipliers), `config/world/world.tres` (resource nodes), `config/display/display.tres` (camera follow). Defaults follow the GDD (180 s minimum before the skip vote, 15 s dusk, waves at 30 s then every 90 s / 45 s, speed and spawn/roamer/drop/node multipliers at 1); raise the multipliers or lower `DayMin` only for local playtests.
- Editable landmarks: open `maps/island.tscn` in Godot's 2D editor. Duplicate `ExampleRock`, move it, choose a nature PNG in Texture (keep Scale at 1 so the pixels stay square), and adjust `Solid Radius` / `Blocks Attacks` in the Inspector. Position is the ground anchor; the script aligns nature textures automatically. Keep these Sprite2D objects as direct children with zero rotation. The procedural forest remains, and every peer reads the same landmark scene. Restart after map edits.
- Combat: solid scenery and intact base walls block melee, enemy sight, tower fire and Spitter projectiles. Projectiles test their complete movement segment and stop at the nearest obstacle/player. Destroyed towers remain physical wrecks. Harvested resource colliders disappear until dawn.
- Presentation: activity states are sent in snapshots; gathering/repair show a swinging tool, healing/reviving show a pulsing cross, and an attack plays the sprite frames of the weapon in hand once (`src/gameplay/view/Main.Anim.cs`); a knocked-down player falls over and lies still until revived; enemies show their attack frames on each hit or spit (snapshot field 9), a boss holds its wind-up frame until the move lands, and a dead enemy falls where it died, lies there and fades (`src/ai/view/Main.EnemyView.cs`). Bosses are drawn at 2x their 48 px sheets (about 3 tiles tall, three times a player) with a 1.1-tile body radius, so they still fit through a door gap. From outside the base you cannot see in: a flat roof of riveted slabs in the walls' cream concrete, rusting at the seams, with vent grates covers it between the wall tops, nobody and nothing inside is drawn, and the lighthouse rises through the roof (it is drawn taller than the walls and roof, so it is seen from outside); the roof fades away when you step inside (`src/world/view/Main.BaseFog.cs`). The lighthouse fades while you stand behind it (`src/world/view/Main.BaseProps.cs`). Animation timing restarts when the state changes. Existing character PNGs are preserved. Camera follow uses a dead zone and exponential smoothing, snapped to whole screen pixels; your own character moves every rendered frame. The walk cycle bounces the body a pixel per stride (10 fps). A resource node breaks bit by bit as it is gathered instead of shrinking (5 units, each unit taken while someone holds E): every unit taken swaps in the next of four broken stages (`art/nature/<node>_dmg<k>_<variant>.png`, drawn by `tools/PixelNature.cs`: pieces knocked out, cracks, chips on the ground), the node jolts and a burst of chips in its colours flies off; while it is being worked it shakes and throws small chips (`src/world/view/Main.NodeBreak.cs`).
- F3 toggles collision outlines for playtesting. Enemy separation and scenery queries use spatial buckets; the renderer culls against the actual camera view at every zoom level.
- Geometry checks: `godot --headless --path . tests/GeometryCheck.tscn` (shared scenery, navigation, swept hits, blocked attacks and movement validation).
- `-- --autolobby` opens the crew lobby with 2 bots for UI previews (`--bots=N` to change). The menu and lobby also support `--shot` screenshots.
- Playtest helpers (with `--autoplay`): `--kit` (materials, a bow and arrows, all recipes unlocked), `--arsenal` (stone spear, titanium blade, hammer and bow in hotbar slots 1-4, to try each attack), `--walls` (two built walls by door A), `--goto=<x>,<y>` (teleport to that tile, e.g. `--goto=6,32` for the outer forest; `--goto=wood|stone|herb|titanium` next to the nearest node of that kind; `--goto=lm:<1-18>` in front of that landmark's structure), `--gathered=0..5` (every node left with that many units, to see the broken stages), `--panel=bag|craft|build|pause|map`, `--zoom=0..2` (camera zoom step, 0 widest), `--night` (straight to night 1 with no waves; after `--goto` into the base the Engineer stays inside), `--boss=0..6` (night 2 next to that boss: Juggernaut, Broodmother, Bio-Cannon, Burrower, Gatebreaker, Phantom, Ironhide).
- `-- --autoplay` (or the old `--autohost`) starts a match at once with 2 bot teammates (`--bots=N` to change, 0 for none); add `--night` to jump to night 1.
- `--shot=<file.png>` saves a screenshot after `--shot-at=<seconds>` (default 4) and quits; `--at=<x>,<y>` puts you at that tile first.
- F9 (host, debug build) ends the current phase.
- Rules check: `godot --headless --path . tests/SimCheck.tscn`

## Art tools (C#)
- Atlas set dressing: `tools/PixelLandmarkDetails.cs` adds 27 sprites to `art/landmarks/`, using the existing pixel canvas and palettes. `src/world/WorldLandmarkDetails.cs` places them: coconut harvest and palms (02), ammonite slab (03), flower borders (04), stripped panels and recovery cable (05), radio/generator depot (06), abandoned convoy checkpoint (07), bank ferns (08), satellite dish/solar panel/windsock (09), fishing boat and buoys (10), trail cairns (11), telescope (12), loading crane (13), anchor and tender (14), pond-bank plants (15), survey cache (16), coastal telescope/wind marker (17), vine arch/rain barrel/pots (18), and exterior service equipment at the outpost (01). These are scenery, not new usable stations or loot. Added solid props are kept off routes and water; the garden arch is open between its posts.
- Landmark ground treatments and wildlife: `src/world/view/Main.LandmarkDetails.cs` adds sand/chalk/soil clearings, tyre marks, meadow butterflies, pond dragonflies and swamp/night fireflies. Ground and tyre marks are cached in one texture per location, built incrementally with a 1 ms CPU target per rendered frame outside `_Draw()`. `LandmarkGroundMask` limits terrain queries to nearby route segments. `--profile-landmarks` reports bake work and the largest time slice; `tests/LandmarkGroundCheck.tscn` compares the filtered queries against the terrain rules.
- Atlas visual review: `godot --path . tools/LandmarkPreview.tscn -- --out=res://.tmp/landmark-review` saves 18 HUD-free screenshots using the actual game renderer. Add `--landmark=9` for one location or `--night` for night lighting. It also checks that the large new props were placed and every referenced landmark sprite exists. Normal gameplay still uses `main.tscn`.
- Props and built walls: `godot --headless --path . tools/PixelProps.tscn`
- Characters: `godot --headless --path . tools/PixelChars.tscn`
- Bosses (7 sheets, `art/chars/boss_*.png` + `bosses.json`): `godot --headless --path . tools/PixelBosses.tscn`
- Item icons (`art/items/<item id>.png`) and projectiles (`art/fx/`): `godot --headless --path . tools/PixelItems.tscn`
- Nature, resource nodes and the ground texture (`art/nature/` + `index.json`), and the landmarks (`art/landmarks/` + `index.json`): `godot --headless --path . tools/PixelNature.tscn` (add `-- --landmarks-only` for just the landmarks)
- Animation viewer (not headless): `godot --path . tools/AnimViewer.tscn` plays every animation of every character and boss in all 8 directions, with a frame-by-frame strip and the feet anchor marked. Left/Right character, Up/Down animation, Z/X strip direction, Space pause, `,` `.` step, `-` `+` speed. Start elsewhere with `-- --char=wrecker --anim=down`.
- Every generator accepts `-- --out=<directory>` to preview output without replacing the shipped art. After adding new PNGs, open the project in the editor once (or run `godot --headless --path . --import`) so Godot imports them.
